# Sales scripts: large accounts and the people who serve them

Written 2026-09-27 for one founder selling [service] to wealthy individuals
and the professionals around them: family office staff, wealth advisors,
chiefs of staff, founder-CEOs. The services are not final, so scripts use
[service], [outcome], and [gap] as placeholders. Section 10 fills them in
for **bespoke private software for a family office**. Every script respects
the facts: one person, Claude Code, Next.js and Vercel, no team, no named
clients.

## 1. Principles for this buyer

1. Discretion first. Never name who else you have spoken to, and say so
   when asked.
2. Brevity. Openers under fifteen seconds; answers under forty words unless
   they ask for more.
3. No flattery. Do not comment on their success, their office, or their name.
4. No name-dropping. You have no clients to drop and would not drop them if
   you did.
5. Ask before telling. A question about their current state precedes any
   sentence about what you do.
6. The gatekeeper is a peer. The chief of staff decides whether you exist;
   for the first call they are the buyer.
7. State price bands plainly and without apology. This buyer distrusts a
   seller who flinches at a number.
8. Never pretend to have clients, results, or a team. "We are new and
   small" is said once, early, in a level voice.
9. A no is information, not a wall. Thank them, record it, leave the door
   on the latch.
10. Write down what they said, in their words, within ten minutes.

## 2. Referral ask

To an existing contact who knows such people. 60 to 90 seconds spoken.

> I want to ask you something and it is fine to say no. I am spending this
> quarter talking to family offices and the advisors around them about
> [service]. The problem I keep hearing about is [gap]; what I do is
> [outcome]. I am one person, so I am looking for three or four
> conversations, not a pipeline. Is there anyone who has mentioned [gap] to
> you, where an introduction would not cost you anything? If not, no harm.
> If so, I will send you two lines you can forward, and they can ignore it
> without awkwardness for you.

Two-line email, for the contact to forward:

> [Founder] builds private software for family offices, one office at a
> time, and is a one-person firm. If [gap] is a live question for you, a
> twenty-minute call is worth having; if not, please ignore this.

## 3. Gatekeeper first call

**Opener, under 15 seconds**

> Good morning, this is [Founder]. I run a small firm that builds [service]
> for family offices. I do not know if this is relevant to you, and it will
> take me a minute to find out. Is now a bad time?

**Permission question**

> May I ask two or three questions about how the office handles [area]
> today? If it is not a fit, I will say so and get off the phone.

**Three discovery questions, current state**

1. "How does the office handle [area] at the moment: what tools, and who
   owns it?" Maps the environment.
2. "When something has to change, who makes the change, and how long does
   it take?" Surfaces the bottleneck.
3. "What happens when that person is away?" Surfaces dependency risk
   without leading.

**The gap question**

> If that were working exactly as the principal wanted, what would be
> different from today?

**"We are new and small," said with confidence**

> I should be clear about what we are. I am one person. I build the system
> myself, I am the person you call, and I take on a small number of offices
> at a time. For some people that is the point; for others it rules us out.
> Which is it for you?

**Close to a 20-minute meeting**

> From what you have said, [restate the gap in their words]. I would like
> twenty minutes with you, or with whoever owns that decision, to map it
> properly. I will not present anything; I will ask questions and tell you
> honestly whether I can help. Would Tuesday or Thursday suit?

**Graceful exit if no**

> Understood. Thank you for the time. I will send one email with my details
> in case [gap] becomes live, and I will not follow up beyond that unless
> you ask.

## 4. Principal or decision-maker discovery call, 20 minutes

**Agenda in one sentence**

> I would like to spend most of this call understanding how [area] works for
> you today, then tell you honestly whether [service] fits, and if it does,
> agree what happens next.

**Current-state questions**

- "Walk me through [area] as it runs today, from the point something
  changes to the point you see the result." *For:* the process in their words.
- "Who touches it, and who is the one person it cannot run without?"
  *For:* the single point of failure.
- "What have you already tried, and why did it not stick?" *For:* what has
  been rejected, so you do not propose it again.

**Impact and quantification questions**

- "When it goes wrong or slowly, what does that cost: in hours, decisions
  delayed, exposure?" *For:* the size of the gap in their currency.
- "How often does that happen?" *For:* frequency times cost is the number
  they will use to justify spending.
- "If nothing changed for another year, what would that mean for you
  personally?" *For:* the personal stake. Pause after asking.

**Decision-process questions**

- "Who else would need to be comfortable before this went ahead?" *For:*
  the advisor, spouse, trustee, or CFO.
- "Is there a date this needs to be solved by, or is it open?" *For:* a
  real timeline or none; never manufacture one.
- "What would have to be true for you to say yes to a one-person firm doing
  this?" *For:* the objection surfaced before the proposal.

**Summary-back**

> Let me check I have this right. Today [current state, their words]. The
> cost is [impact, their words]. The people who would need to agree are
> [roles]. The timeline is [their answer]. Have I missed anything?

**Next step**

> I write a two-page scope with a price band and send it by [day]. We then
> spend fifteen minutes walking through it. If it is wrong, you tell me and
> we stop. Does that work?

## 5. Objection handling

| Objection | What it usually means | Response, under 40 words | Follow-up question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| We already have someone | Loyalty, or fear of switching cost | Good. I am not asking you to replace them. I am asking whether there is a gap they have not had time for. | What is the one thing they keep saying they will get to? |
| Send me something | Polite exit, or a real need to show a colleague | I will, and it will be two paragraphs written for you, not a brochure. May I ask one question first so it is useful? | What would the person you show it to need to see? |
| Too small | Doubt about capacity and continuity | Fair. I take on few offices for that reason, and the system is built in your accounts, so it does not depend on me to keep running. | What would you need to see to be comfortable with that? |
| What have you built for people like us | Wants proof; also a discretion test | Nothing I can name, and I would not name it if I could. I can show you my own work and walk you through how I would build yours. | Would a working example on a sample of your data answer that? |
| Price | Value not yet clear, or budget owner absent | The band is [low] to [high], depending on scope. I say it plainly because you will decide on value, not on the number. | Which part of the scope matters most to you? |
| Security and confidentiality | Real concern about data leaving their control | Nothing runs on my servers. Code, database, and hosting sit in accounts you own, and access is yours to revoke. I sign whatever your counsel requires. | Who sets the security requirements in the office? |
| Not now | Priority, not rejection | Understood. May I ask what is ahead of it, so I know whether to come back? | When would be a sensible time to check in, if ever? |
| My advisor handles that | Deference, or a real gatekeeper | Then they are the right person to speak to. May I contact them, mentioning we spoke? | Would you prefer to introduce us, or shall I reach out directly? |
| We do not take cold calls | A boundary, to be respected | Understood, and I will not call again. May I send one email so you have my details if [gap] ever comes up? | Is email acceptable, or would you rather I did not? |
| What is your background | Credibility check on a stranger | I build software and AI systems with Claude Code, Next.js, and Vercel, and I run a small storefront I built. Before that, [owner fills in]. One person by choice. | What in that would you want to know more about? |
| We would build it in-house | Has technical staff, or wants control | That may be right. I build in your repository, so anything I start, your people can finish. | Who in-house would own it, and do they have the time? |
| How do we know you will be around | Continuity risk | You do not, and I will not pretend otherwise. That is why it lives in your accounts, documented so any developer can read it. | Would a handover plan written into the contract help? |

## 7. Proposal walk-through call, 15 minutes

**Scope.** Read it aloud in their words from discovery, one line per
deliverable. Ask "anything missing or unnecessary" after each section.

**Price.** State the figure once, flat: "The price for that scope is
[price], paid [terms]." Then stop.

**The pause rule.** After the price, say nothing until they speak. Ten
seconds of silence is normal. The first thing they say is the real objection
or the real yes; do not fill the gap with justification.

**Terms.** Payment schedule, ownership (everything, theirs, from the first
commit), what happens if either side stops, the handover document. Two
minutes.

**Asking for the decision**

> You have the scope, the price, and the terms. What would you like to do?

Yes: agree the start date and first payment before ending the call. No:
"Thank you for hearing it through. May I ask what decided it?" Record the
answer. Undecided: "What would you need to know that you do not know now,
and by when will you know it?" Set the date to speak again.

## 8. Post-call

**Five-line notes template**

```
Account / person / role:
Current state, their words:
Gap and cost, their words:
Who else decides, and timeline:
Next action, date, owner:
```

**Same-day follow-up email, under 120 words**

> Subject: Notes from today
>
> Thank you for the time. What I heard: [current state, one sentence].
> The cost as you described it: [one sentence]. The people who would need
> to agree: [roles]. What I will do: [next action] by [date]. If I have
> anything wrong, please correct me; I would rather know now.
>
> [Founder]

**Two-week rhythm**

- Day 0: call, notes, follow-up email.
- Day 2: the promised document, exactly as promised, nothing extra.
- Day 7: one short note, only if there is new information for them.
- Day 14: one call or email asking whether timing has changed. No reply:
  move to re-contact in ninety days and stop.

## 9. Call list format

Names are never invented; the list is built from research and referrals.

**Daily call block.** Twelve dials in ninety minutes, in this order:

1. Warm referrals (up to four): every introduction goes first, the day it
   arrives.
2. Signal-based accounts (up to six): a public signal such as a new
   operations hire, a stated technology project, or a founder exit in the
   press.
3. Re-contacts (the rest): anyone who said "not now" whose date has come.

Voicemail on the first dial; never two voicemails in one week.

**Pre-call research card, five fields, two minutes**

```
Who they are and role (one line):
The signal that put them on the list (one line, with source):
Likely gap, in my words (to be replaced by theirs):
Who probably decides (role):
The one question I will open with:
```

**Log columns**, matching `docs/sales/target-accounts.csv`:

| account | segment | signal | stage | next_action | next_action_date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| office or firm | family office, wealth advisor, chief of staff, founder-CEO | observed signal, with source | new, contacted, gatekeeper, discovery, proposal, won, lost, re-contact | the specific action | ISO date |

## 10. Worked example: family office software

The founder is honest throughout that this is a one-person firm.

### Gatekeeper call

**Founder:** Good morning, this is [Founder]. I run a small firm that builds
private software for family offices. I do not know if this is relevant to
you, and it will take me a minute to find out. Is now a bad time?

**Chief of staff:** You have two minutes.

**Founder:** Thank you. May I ask two or three questions about how the
office handles its reporting? How does consolidated reporting run at the
moment: what tools, and who owns it?

**Chief of staff:** Spreadsheets. Our controller maintains them. Custodians
send statements, she reconciles, I assemble the quarterly pack.

**Founder:** When something changes, a new entity or custodian, who makes
the change and how long does it take?

**Chief of staff:** She does. A new entity is a week of her time, and errors
creep in for a quarter afterwards.

**Founder:** And when she is away?

**Chief of staff:** Nothing moves. We have talked about that.

**Founder:** If it worked exactly as the principal wanted, what would be
different?

**Chief of staff:** He would open one page and see the whole picture, and it
would not depend on one person.

**Founder:** I should be clear about what we are. I am one person. I build
the system myself, I am the person you call, and I take on a small number of
offices at a time. For some people that is the point; for others it rules us
out. Which is it for you?

**Chief of staff:** Depends where the data sits.

**Founder:** In your accounts; I have access only while you grant it. From
what you have said, the gap is a reporting process that depends on one
person and breaks for a quarter when structure changes. I would like twenty
minutes with you and whoever owns that decision to map it properly. I will
not present anything. Would Tuesday or Thursday suit?

**Chief of staff:** Thursday, with me first. Send me an email.

**Founder:** Two lines, today. Thank you.

### Discovery call

**Founder:** Thank you for the time. I would like to spend most of it
understanding how reporting runs today, then tell you honestly whether what I
do fits, and if it does, agree what happens next. Does that work?

**Chief of staff:** Yes.

**Founder:** Walk me through a quarter, from the statements arriving to the
principal seeing the pack.

**Chief of staff:** Statements land over two weeks. The controller keys
positions into the master workbook, reconciles to last quarter, and I build
the summary. The principal gets a PDF about five weeks after quarter end.

**Founder:** Who is the one person it cannot run without?

**Chief of staff:** The controller. There is logic in that workbook nobody
else understands.

**Founder:** What have you tried already, and why did it not stick?

**Chief of staff:** Two portfolio platforms. Both wanted the data on their
servers and neither handled the private holdings. The principal would not
have it.

**Founder:** When it goes wrong or slowly, what does it cost: in hours,
decisions delayed, exposure?

**Chief of staff:** Hours I could estimate. The real cost is that he decides
on numbers five weeks old, and twice we found errors after he had acted.

**Founder:** How often does the structure change enough to break it?

**Chief of staff:** Two or three times a year.

**Founder:** If nothing changed for another year, what would that mean for
you personally?

**Chief of staff:** (pause) That I am still the person explaining the
mistake.

**Founder:** Who else would need to be comfortable before this went ahead?

**Chief of staff:** The principal, and outside counsel on anything touching
data.

**Founder:** Is there a date this needs to be solved by?

**Chief of staff:** Before the annual review, in about seven months.

**Founder:** What would have to be true for you to say yes to a one-person
firm doing this?

**Chief of staff:** That it does not die if you do.

**Founder:** That is the right test. Everything I build sits in your GitHub,
your database, and your hosting, documented so any developer can take it
over, and I write the handover into the contract. Let me check I have this
right. Today, a workbook one person understands, a five-week lag, errors
found after decisions. Two or three structural changes a year, each costing
a week and a quarter of clean-up. The principal and counsel must agree, and
the deadline is the annual review. Have I missed anything?

**Chief of staff:** No, that is it.

**Founder:** Then I write a two-page scope with a price band, honest about
what a first version does and does not do, and send it by Monday. We spend
fifteen minutes walking through it with you, and with the principal if you
judge it ready. If it is wrong, you tell me and we stop. Does that work?

**Chief of staff:** Monday. Yes.

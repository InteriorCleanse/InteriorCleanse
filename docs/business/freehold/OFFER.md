# Offer: Wedge A, "your repo, your database, your key"

Written 2026-09-26 from STRATEGY.md. Company name not stated; "[Name]" stands
in. Every Base44 figure below is from STRATEGY.md, which took it from a search
snippet; open the source before repeating it in public. No product exists yet,
so nothing here is a result. VOICE_OF_BUILDER.md does not exist; where a

section 2.

## 1. The core offer

Describe the app you want. Within the session it exists as a Next.js
repository in your GitHub, a Postgres database in your account, and a
deployment on your Vercel, generated by Claude on your own Anthropic key.
[Name] never hosts your app, never holds your data, and never meters your
usage; you pay [Name] a flat monthly fee for the orchestration, and you pay
Anthropic directly for the model, on a bill you can read. If the AI cannot
finish a build, that build costs you nothing. If you leave, you take
everything, because everything was already yours.

**Value equation**

- **Dream outcome.** An app that runs whether or not [Name] does. Tied to
  complaint 2, lock-in ("frontend comes out, backend stays behind Base44's
  SDK", perpet.io, 6 sources) and complaint 6, outages ("no SLA at any tier,
  the affected apps had no recourse but to wait", medium.com/@jacobp96, 4
  sources). The buyer is not buying an app builder; they are buying the end
  of dependence.
- **Perceived likelihood.** The buyer has already watched one AI fail. We do
  not claim ours will not. We claim the failure is visible (it is a commit in
  your repo, readable by any developer) and unbilled (complaint 1, "you spend
  five additional credits fixing something that wasn't your fault",
  shipper.now, 7 sources; complaint 5, "constantly breaks its own code and is
  not capable of fixing it", Trustpilot, 5 sources). Proof is structural, not
  testimonial: the repo, the key, the database, the bill.
- **Time delay.** First working deployment within the session (STRATEGY
  section 4A). Contrast is the Base44 exit, where "an eject starts with an
  empty database" (section 1, corroborated) and the work of rebuilding auth
  and data logic begins after export. Here there is no exit to plan because
  there is no entry.
- **Effort and sacrifice.** Honest cost: the buyer must create a GitHub
  account, a Postgres account, a Vercel account, and an Anthropic key. That is
  four sign-ups Base44 does not ask for. [Name] absorbs the rest: connecting
  them, generating schema, auth, and pages, deploying, and answering the
  ticket with a human (complaint 3, "the helpdesk is again the same AI
  responding", Trustpilot, 5 sources). Cancellation is one click and the
  refund policy is one sentence (complaint 4, charges after cancelling, 5
  sources).

Price sits in the band STRATEGY names ($20 to $25 a month at Base44 Starter,
Lovable Pro, Bolt Pro, Replit Core; zero at Dyad), with the model bill the
customer's. The owner picks the point; write "[price]" until then.

## 2. Guarantees

**Make from day one, because they are true by construction:**

1. *You are never charged for a build the AI could not finish.* Wedge B's
   billing rule, folded in. It is affordable only because the model cost is on
   the customer's key; [Name] forgoes its own fee for the failed build, not the
   token spend. State that plainly.
2. *Your repo is yours the minute it exists.* The first commit lands in the
   customer's GitHub, the schema in their Postgres, the deployment on their
   Vercel. [Name] cannot withhold what it never held.
3. *Cancel in one click; the refund policy is one sentence.* The sentence is
   the owner's to write; a candidate: "Cancel any time; the current month is
   not refunded, nothing after it is charged."

**Do not make yet, and why:**

- *Uptime.* Two different things could be promised and neither can be. The
  customer's app runs on Vercel, whose availability is Vercel's to state. The
  [Name] builder itself has no operating history, and a solo founder has no
  on-call rota. Say instead: "your app does not run on our servers, so our
  outage is not yours." That is true and is the point.
- *Migration completeness.* Base44's export is frontend-only; "auth, database
  logic, and hosted runtime stay behind the Base44 SDK" (section 1,
  corroborated). Nobody can promise to reconstruct what never left, and the
  import path does not exist yet (STRATEGY section 5 calls it the condition for
  the gap staying open). Promise an honest audit of what is missing, not a
  complete migration.

## 3. Lead magnets, ranked

### 1. The free Base44 export audit (Solve)

- **Visitor gets.** A written report on their own export: which files came
  out, every call into the Base44 SDK, what the app needs that is absent
  (auth, data model, backend functions, integrations), and what it would take
  to run independently. Useful whether or not they buy; a freelancer could work
  from it.
- **Founder cost (illustration).** Early on, the founder reads each export
  with Claude Code and writes the report by hand; expect an hour or more per
  audit. After the first few, the SDK-call scan becomes a script and the
  hand-written part shrinks to the summary.
- **Proves.** That [Name] understands the lock-in at file level, and that the
  migration path is a real piece of work someone is doing, not a claim.
- **Fields.** Email; GitHub repository link or ZIP upload; "what does the app
  do, in one sentence"; optional: current Base44 plan.
- **Follow-up email.** One paragraph, from the founder's name, attaching the
  report: what was found, the three biggest gaps, one sentence on what [Name]
  would do with it, and a plain offer of a reply if they have questions. No
  sequence yet; one human email.

### 2. The "leaving Base44" checklist (Educate)

- **Visitor gets.** A one-page checklist: export the ZIP and CSVs, confirm
  GitHub sync is on the plan that allows it (Builder or higher, section 1),
  inventory integrations and backend functions, cancel before the renewal
  date, keep the confirmation. Written from the sources in STRATEGY section 6,
  each step cited, each source opened first.
- **Founder cost (illustration).** Written once; an afternoon plus source
  checking. Revised when Base44 changes its export.
- **Proves.** That [Name] will tell the truth about the competitor's product
  even where it is adequate.
- **Fields.** Email only.
- **Follow-up email.** The checklist as a link, one paragraph explaining the
  one item most people miss (the database does not export as logic, only as
  CSV), and an offer of the audit above.

### 3. The public build log (Sample)

- **Visitor gets.** A page where every app [Name] builds for a consenting
  customer is logged: prompt count, what broke, what was unbilled, link to the
  public repo where the customer allows it.
- **Founder cost (illustration).** One entry per build; minutes each, but
  forever.
- **Proves.** The billing rule in the open, which is the one promise Base44
  has in writing that it will not match ("non-refundable for tool behavior and
  AI mistakes", docs.base44.com, unverified wording).
- **Fields.** Email, to be told when a new entry lands.
- **Follow-up email.** The latest entry, one paragraph, no pitch.

Ship 1 first; it is also the sales conversation. Ship 2 the same week because
it is cheap. Ship 3 only once there is a build to log; before that it is empty
and must not be published.

## 4. First channel and first ten customers

One Core Four channel: **warm and cold outreach, by hand, on the platforms
STRATEGY names.** No paid, no agency, no affiliate until ten customers exist.
Per BUSINESS_BUILD_PLAN rules, nothing is posted or emailed by an agent; the
founder presses every button. The Rule of 100 applies as a daily count of
genuine, individual replies and proposals, not as a broadcast.

**Fiverr and Upwork, "fix my Base44 app" gigs.** The gig sellers are
competitors and, later, possible partners; do not message them. The buyers
posting jobs are the customers. Approach: become a seller yourself, offering
the audit and the migration as a service, priced and paid on the platform.
Both marketplaces forbid taking contact or payment off-platform; check the
current terms before the first proposal and keep the whole job on-platform.
Proposal, in the buyer's own words: "You have the frontend but the backend
stayed behind the Base44 SDK, so the export starts with an empty database. I
will audit what is missing and rebuild it as a Next.js repo in your GitHub with
a Postgres database in your account. You keep everything; if I cannot finish,
you do not pay for that build." The first ten customers are probably these,
served by hand, before [Name] the product exists.

**Trustpilot one-star reviewers.** Do not contact them. Trustpilot exposes no
contact details and its reply function belongs to Base44's profile, not ours.
Use the reviews as vocabulary for the landing page and the checklist, and rely
on those pages being found by the same searches the reviewers ran.

**r/vibecoding.** Read the subreddit rules first and follow them exactly. One
post, disclosed as the builder, offering the free audit with no link in the
body if links are disallowed. Then answer, in comments, people asking about
Base44 exports with the actual answer, whether or not it leads anywhere. Post
text: "I am building a tool that puts the whole app, backend included, in your
own GitHub, database, and Vercel. Before it exists I am auditing Base44 exports
for free to learn what is missing. If you have one, I will write up what would
take to run it on your own. Not a pitch; I want the data." Never a second
account, never a planted question.

**escapebase44.com readers.** The site is a third party. Email its owner
directly, once, disclosing the plan, offering the checklist and audit as
resources they may link if they judge them useful, and asking nothing in
return. If they decline, read it for vocabulary and move on.

Measure weekly: audits requested, audits delivered, conversations that turn
into a paid build. Not impressions.

## 5. Landing page copy

**Headline.** Your app, in your repo, on your key.

**Subhead.** Describe it once. Get a Next.js repo in your GitHub, a Postgres
database in your account, a deployment on your Vercel.

**Proof points (true of the model today):**

- Your repo. The first commit is in your GitHub before you have paid anything.
- Your bill. The model runs on your Anthropic key; you read that bill, not us.
- Your database and your deployment. Nothing runs on our servers, so our bad
  day is not yours.

**CTA.** Send us your Base44 export. We will tell you what is missing, free.

**FAQ**

*Do I need to know how to code?* No, but you need four accounts: GitHub,
Postgres, Vercel, Anthropic. We walk you through each. It is more setup than
Base44 asks for, and it is the reason you own the result.

*What happens when the AI breaks something?* It will, sometimes. The break is
a commit you can see, any developer can read, and we can retry. A build we
cannot finish is not billed by us. Your Anthropic key is still charged for the
tokens; we do not control that meter and will not pretend to.

*Can you import my Base44 app?* Not fully, and neither can anyone. Base44's
export is the frontend; auth and database logic stay behind its SDK. We audit
what came out, rebuild what did not, and tell you the difference in writing
before you pay.

*What does it cost?* [price] a month to us, plus whatever your Anthropic usage
costs, on your own bill. No credits, no integration meter.

*What if you disappear?* Your app keeps running. It is on your Vercel, your
database, your repo. You would lose the tool that builds the next version, not
the version you have.

*How do I cancel?* One click on the account page. [Refund sentence]. We do
not do email, SMS, native mobile, or an integration store; if you need those,
Base44 has them and we do not.

# Picked expansion: drinks and supplements

**The rule that keeps the brand worth something:** every Picked product is
flavored mainly by real fruit, or carries no flavor at all. A stock "berry
blast" formula with a Picked label would undo the one promise competitors
can't copy. Products outside that rule can go under a separate brand later.

**The order:** protein first, then real-fruit hydration sticks, then
creatine, then greens or collagen with fruit, then a ready-to-drink protein
water. Each wave starts only when the one before meets its gate, so sales
fund the next product.

Figures come from manufacturer pages and guides found by web search in
October 2026 (the pages themselves were blocked from the research
environment). Confirm every MOQ and price with the manufacturer before
paying. Sources are at the end.

## The big change: low minimums exist

**Pure Private Label** (Georgia) makes custom and stock powders from **144
units**, with samples of up to three custom flavors for **$695** ($500 of it
credited to the first order) and about **8 to 10 weeks** from approved label
to finished goods. It makes sports nutrition and hydration products.

- **For protein:** ask whether it can make a clear whey with freeze-dried
  fruit. If it can, the first Strawberry run drops from 1,000 pouches (about
  $23k) to 144 pouches (an estimated $3.5k to $5k, since small runs cost more
  per unit). That makes a real test launch possible for under $10k all in.
- **For the next products:** the same 144-unit door opens hydration sticks,
  flavored creatine, and greens.

Add Pure Private Label to the first round of quote requests in
`LAUNCH_GUIDE.md`. It is the first call to make.

## Fast but careful: products before the protein is ready

**Supliful** holds stock formulas, prints your label, and ships each order
when a customer buys, with no minimum. It plugs into Shopify. A paid
membership runs about $49 to $69 a month (sources disagree), and you pay per
order.

| Supliful product | Their cost per unit | Usable for Picked? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creatine monohydrate, 250 g (about 50 servings) | $9.19 to $14.69 | **Yes, unflavored.** No fruit claim needed. A real store and real orders weeks before protein ships |
| Hydration powder (passion fruit) | $8.05 to $14.79 | **No.** Stock flavor, so no "real fruit" claim. Use only to test demand under a plain label, or skip |
| Whey isolate (chocolate) | $24.84 to $32.89 | **No.** Off-brand flavor |
| Greens, collagen, gummies, pre-workout | various | **No.** Stock formulas without fruit |

**Age rule first:** New York bans selling creatine to anyone under 18 and
requires an ID check at delivery for online sales. California passed a
similar law that likely starts January 1, 2027. Selling creatine means an age
check at checkout and adult-signature delivery to those states, or not
shipping it there. See `LEGAL.md`.

**Recommendation:** use Supliful for one product only: **Picked Creatine,
unflavored** ("mixes into anything, including your Picked"). It lets the
Shopify store, payments, emails, and reviews run on real orders from about
week 3. Retire it once a real-fruit creatine exists. Supliful can't change
formulas below an Enterprise plan with $10,000 upfront, so nothing from its
catalog can carry the fruit promise.

Other no-minimum platforms checked (Rocktomic, HoneyComm, Vox Nutrition's
on-demand program, Vitalabs, Blanka) also sell stock formulas only.

## Beverages: what to know before spending

- **A clear protein water** (whey isolate acidified to about pH 3.3 to 3.7)
  can be hot-filled and stays shelf-stable without the federal low-acid canned
  food rules. That is the cheap road into drinks, and fruit flavors suit it.
- **A milky protein shake** (neutral pH) is a low-acid food. It needs retort
  or aseptic processing, an FDA process filing, and runs that cost $30k to
  $150k minimum. Picked should not make one.
- **Cold-filled juices** need high-pressure processing and a cold chain from
  plant to fridge (Universal Pure: 20,000-bottle minimum). Skip.
- **Mobile canning** ($3 to $4.80 a case) is built for breweries and
  carbonated acidic drinks, not protein.
- Ready-to-drink beverages are usually regulated as conventional foods, not
  supplements, and some states charge container deposits. See `LEGAL.md`.

## Not recommended

| Product | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| Gummies | Minimums of 1,000 to 5,000 bottles and flavor-system limits make real fruit hard. Heat and humidity cause returns |
| Protein bars | Real fruit inclusions are possible, but custom minimums are about 10,000 bars. A different factory and a different shelf |
| Pre-workout | Caffeine and stimulant blends bring warning labels, state age rules, and a gym-bro image the brand avoids |
| Weight-loss or "burn" products | State laws restrict sales to minors, FTC scrutiny is heavy, and it contradicts the brand voice |
| Anything without fruit and with a flavor | Breaks the core promise. Launch it under another name if it's worth doing |

## Gates between waves

Move to the next wave only when all three are true:

1. **Repeat:** at least 25% of first-time buyers reorder within 60 days.
2. **Cash:** the next wave's cash need is covered by profit or committed
   funding, with 3 months of fixed costs left over.
3. **Capacity:** the current product ships on time with no open quality
   issues.

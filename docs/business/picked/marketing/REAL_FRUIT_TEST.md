# The Real Fruit Test

**How to read a protein label in 60 seconds.**

A fruit picture on the front doesn't mean fruit in the tub. Turn it around, run these five checks, and you'll know in about a minute.

## 1. The flavor name

- **Look for:** the exact words next to the fruit name on the front.
- **What it means:** US rules (21 CFR 101.22(i)) set this wording. Plain "Strawberry" is allowed when there's enough real strawberry to carry the taste. If not, the name has to say so: "natural strawberry flavored", "strawberry flavored with other natural flavor" (often shortened to WONF), or "artificially flavored strawberry".
- **Red flag:** small print after the fruit name.
- **Green flag:** just the fruit. Nothing after it.

## 2. The ingredient list

- **Look for:** where the fruit sits, and the words "natural flavors".
- **What it means:** ingredients run from most to least by weight. "Natural flavors" is a broad term. It doesn't tell you which fruit, or whether any fruit is in there at all.
- **Red flag:** fruit near the end, behind natural flavors and sweeteners. Or juice that's only there for color.
- **Green flag:** the fruit named near the top, like "freeze-dried strawberry". Bonus if the pack gives grams.

## 3. The sweetener

- **Look for:** sucralose, acesulfame potassium (Ace-K), stevia (often listed as Reb M), monk fruit.
- **What it means:** most clear proteins use a no-calorie sweetener. Which one shapes the aftertaste.
- **Red flag:** "fruit" all over the front, sucralose and Ace-K on the back.
- **Green flag:** one or two sweeteners, named plainly.

## 4. The protein number

- **Look for:** the protein line on the Supplement Facts or Nutrition Facts panel, and its %DV.
- **What it means:** if the front says "20g protein", the panel has to show a % Daily Value for protein, corrected for protein quality using PDCAAS (protein digestibility-corrected amino acid score). Whey's PDCAAS is 1.0.
- **Red flag:** a big protein number on the front, no %DV for protein on the back.
- **Green flag:** grams and %DV both shown, and they match the front.

## 5. The lab results

- **Look for:** a lot number plus a QR code or link to a certificate of analysis (COA).
- **What it means:** a COA is the lab report for one batch. For protein powder, the line that matters is heavy metals: lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury. In one study of 160 protein powders, 47% exceeded California's Prop 65 safe-harbor level for lead (0.5 µg a day).
- **Red flag:** "lab tested" or "purity guaranteed" with no results you can see.
- **Green flag:** a COA for the lot in your hand, from an accredited lab, with heavy metals listed as numbers.

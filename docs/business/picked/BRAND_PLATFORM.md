# Picked brand platform

`BRAND.md` covers how Picked looks and sounds. This file covers what Picked
stands for, the promises it keeps in public, and the brand assets that build
on each other. Every campaign, product, and hire should trace back to it.

## The one idea

**Real fruit, proven.** Everyone says "real fruit". Picked prints the grams
and posts the lab results. The brand wins by being the protein you never have
to take on faith.

## Why Picked exists

Fruit-flavored protein has trained people to expect a lie: a strawberry on the
front, "natural and artificial flavors" on the back, and an aftertaste that
tastes like neither. Picked exists to make the label and the taste tell the
same story.

**What we're against:** fake fruit. Not other brands, not other people's
choices. Fake fruit flavor, vague labels, and promises you can't check.

## The Picked Standard

Five public promises. They go on the pack, on the site, and in every pitch.
They are written so anyone can check them, and so breaking one would be
obvious. That is what makes them hard to copy: a competitor would have to
change its formula, not its copy.

1. **The fruit is in it.** Real fruit is the main flavor ingredient in every
   flavored product.
2. **The grams are on the front.** Every pack prints how much real fruit is
   in a serving.
3. **No sucralose. No acesulfame K. No artificial colors.** Sweetened with
   monk fruit and/or stevia, colored by the fruit.
4. **Every lot is tested.** Each batch is tested by an independent lab for
   heavy metals and microbiology before it ships.
5. **The results are public.** Every lot's results are posted in the Lot Book
   at pickedprotein.com/lots, one scan from the QR code on the pack.

**Rule:** a product that can't meet all five doesn't carry the Picked name.
(Unflavored products meet 1 to 3 by having no flavor at all.)

## The brand assets

These are the things people will recognize Picked by. Use them everywhere,
and never change them casually.

| Asset | What it is | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The leaf on the i** | The wordmark's one flourish | Logo, favicon, scoop, stickers |
| **The fruit number** | "[x]g real strawberry" set big on the front | Pack, ads, shelf talker, product page hero |
| **Flavor colors** | Each flavor owns one color | Pack, site switcher, social, stick packs |
| **The Lot Book** | The public page of lot results | QR on pack, site footer, every "is it clean?" reply |

## Community: First Picks

Waitlist members who buy in the founding window are **First Picks**. It costs
nothing to run and gives the earliest customers a name, which they'll use.

- They vote on every new flavor before anyone else.
- Their names (first name and last initial, opt-in only) go on a thank-you
  card in the first batch's boxes.
- They get first access to each new product.
- Never promise a permanent discount, only early access and a say.

## How Picked names things

- **Flavors are the fruit, plainly.** Strawberry, Mango, Raspberry Lemon.
  Never "Berry Blast", "Tropical Storm", or a candy name.
- **Limited runs are seasonal:** "Picked in Season: Summer Peach". Only when
  the fruit is genuinely sourced for it.
- **Product lines describe what they are:** Real Fruit Protein, Real Fruit
  Hydration. No invented ingredient names, no trademarked "blends".

## The story

Every brand needs a founder story, and Picked's must be true. Write it in your
own words from these prompts, then keep it under 120 words:

1. The moment you noticed fruit protein tasted fake. Where were you?
2. What you did about it before starting a company.
3. Why you decided to print the grams and post the lab results.
4. What you want someone to feel on the first sip.

Use it on the About page, in the welcome email, and in the first video. Do not
let anyone, including an AI, write a story you didn't live.

## What great looks like in a year

These are aims, not forecasts.

- People quote the Picked Standard back to you in reviews.
- Other brands start printing fruit grams, and customers say Picked did it first.
- A store buyer asks to see the Lot Book before you show it.
- Repeat customers outnumber first-time customers each month.
